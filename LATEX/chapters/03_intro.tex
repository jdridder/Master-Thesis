\chapter{Introduction}\label{intro} 


Whether finance, science or engineering, optimality is a key ambition in
a vast amount of applications. A specified outcome should be realized with the least amount
of resources or investment. It becomes especially challenging to 
find a trustworthy optimal solution, when unconsidered effects influence the underlying mathematical model.
In many real-world scenarios, circumstances are subject to uncertainty. 
Investments must be taken within insecure markets.
For self-driving cars, a road may inherit varying grip. 
Catalysts degrade and exhibit slower reaction speeds over time.
Thus, \emph{robust optimization} is a major field of research to yield optimality under
uncertain conditions with \emph{robustness} regarding constraints and \emph{performance} regarding the objective. 
The constraints should be satisfied in all cases, while the specified performance
is as good as possible.
\newline
\newline
\noindent
\emph{Model predictive control} (MPC) is an online optimization algorithm that repeatedly 
calculates optimal actions for a system based on a specified objective. With regard to
self-driving cars, autonomous aviation or load-flexible chemical plants, MPC can enable
optimal operation under dynamic conditions. However, uncertainty remains a key challenge
in safe application. \emph{Multi-stage MPC} can handle low-order uncertainties with good performance
in the objective but leads to tremendous computational effort, when the uncertainty set grows \cite{lucia2013}.
\newline
\newline
\noindent
With advancements in parallelization of \emph{neural network} (NN) training, NNs have become
state of the art in data-driven modelling \cite{mcbride2019}. Thanks to the \emph{universal approximation theorem}
\cite{hornik1990} and transformer architectures such as large language models \cite{vaswani2023},
the 2010s caused a breakthrough in machine learning. Therefore, NNs are well suited to replace the physical model
of an MPC algorithm to capture complex system dynamics without extensive integration effort.
Further, they are able to learn the effects of uncertainty on the system behavior. When a distribution
of data points is presented, NNs can be trained to infer confidence intervals and thus quantify uncertainty.
This comes with one downside.
\newline
\newline
\noindent
Due to its black-box-nature, a basic NN cannot obey explicit equations regarding its output vector.
This might be a problem in strict cases, where balance equations such as energy, mass or capital need to be met precisely.
When outputs become propagated through NNs several times, errors accumulate. Exactly this is performed
by MPC algorithms making them prone to the accumulation of errors. To tackle this, a recent type of
activation function is presented equivalently to Chen et al. \cite{chen2024}, that corrects NN outputs based on
linear equality constraints during both forward and backward passes.
\newline
\newline
\noindent
The performance of the yieled \emph{physics-constrained} (PC) NNs is later evaluated in a case study.
Here, a dynamic \emph{plug-flow tubular reactor} (PFTR) of the \emph{ethylene oxidation} is modeled to
imitate a load-flexible process under parameteric uncertainty. It is exhibited through the Gaussian-distributed
kinetic parameters \emph{activation energy} and \emph{pre-exponential collision factor} as well as the \emph{radial heat conductivity}
of the packed bed. A surrogate model framework is supposed to be developed, that 
captures the system uncertainty while yielding accurate predictions for an MPC algorithm. A comparison
is drawn between basic NNs and PCNNs in terms of training efficiency,
physics-consistency, simulation capability, uncertainty quantification and MPC robustness and performance.
This is realized in \texttt{python} with the open-source libraries \texttt{pyTorch} \cite{pytorch2019} for
training, \texttt{l4casadi} \cite{salzmann2023} for hessian-acceleration and interconnection,
\texttt{do-mpc} \cite{dompc} for simulation and control, \texttt{casadi} \cite{casadi}
for numerical optimization and \texttt{ipopt} \cite{Waechter2006} for solving.
